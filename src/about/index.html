Parent Advisory Council

About the PAC

PAC means Parent Advisory Council. The McRoberts PAC is run by parent volunteers. It supports students, works with the school and shares parents' views.

In short

If your child goes to McRoberts, you are a member of the PAC. You do not need to sign up. Parents and guardians meet, vote on how PAC money is spent, and give the school their ideas.

What the PAC is

The BC School Act lets the parents of each public school form one Parent Advisory Council. The McRoberts PAC is the PAC of Hugh McRoberts Secondary in Richmond.

Every parent or guardian of a McRoberts student is a member. You do not need to sign up. There is no fee.

Why the PAC exists

  • To help parents take part in their children's education at McRoberts.
  • To support school staff and work with them so students do well.
  • To help people in the school community talk to each other, respect each other and work together.
  • To give parents a place to talk about the school and get involved in school activities.

This is a short version in plain words. The bylaws' own words are in Our bylaws, below.

What the PAC does

  • Meets. The PAC meets about seven times a year. The principal gives a report and answers parents' questions. See the meeting dates.
  • Funds student activities. A yearly grant from the Province of BC pays for clubs, teams, trips and events that teachers ask for through the Teachers' Wish List. Parents vote on the list at a meeting.
  • Raises money. Family fundraisers pay for things the grant cannot pay for, like Grade 12 scholarships and thanking staff. Ways to give.
  • Supports Dry After Grad. The PAC helps pay for the safe, alcohol-free celebration for graduates. About Dry After Grad.
  • Speaks up. The PAC shares parents' views with the school and with the Richmond District Parents Association (RDPA). In February 2026, the PAC gathered parents' views on the school district's budget for an RDPA survey. Parents said students' learning needs, with enough teachers and staff, should come first.
  • Welcomes new families. The PAC wants new families, especially Grade 8 families, to feel at home.

Members and voting

Everyone is welcome at PAC meetings. Only parents and guardians of McRoberts students can vote.

Can vote

  • Parents and guardians of McRoberts students

Welcome, but cannot vote

  • The principal and vice principals
  • Teachers and other school staff
  • Community members who do not have a child at McRoberts

The bylaws ask members to come to as many meetings as they can, learn about the school's programs, and support the work of the PAC. Come when you can. You can just listen.

Giving advice to the school

The BC School Act gives PACs an advisory role. Through its elected officers, a PAC may give advice to the school board, the principal and staff on any matter about the school. This is in section 8 of the BC School Act.

Most of this happens at PAC meetings. The principal, {{site.school.principal}}, or a staff member the principal sends, comes to every meeting. The principal reports on school programs and answers parents' questions. In 2025–26, parents asked about course changes, a university fair and student competitions.

Richmond School District rules (Regulation 802.4-R) say a PAC should support the leadership of the principal. PAC meetings do not discuss individual staff, students or parents.

The PAC does not handle concerns about one student. For a question about your own child, please contact the school first. Who to contact at the school.

Independent from the school

The McRoberts PAC is run by parent volunteers under the BC School Act. It works closely with the school, but it is a separate group. This is not an official website of Hugh McRoberts Secondary or Richmond School District No. 38.

For school business, attendance or records, contact the school office at {{site.school.phone}} or {{site.school.email}}, or visit the McRoberts school website.

What the main roles do

  • Chair: calls and runs meetings, speaks for the PAC, reviews letters and emails to the PAC, and appoints committee members.
  • Secretary: writes the minutes and drafts letters.
  • Treasurer: keeps the financial records, reports the account balances at each meeting, and follows school board rules for PAC funds.
  • RDPA Representative: goes to district parent meetings and reports back.

Where possible, one person holds one role for no more than two years, then helps the next person.

Our bylaws

The PAC's constitution and bylaws set out how the PAC works. They were first passed in 1995 and last changed on September 11, 1997. Below is a short summary of each part. Any member can ask for a full copy by email.

The quoted text below is the 1997 wording.

Mandate, purpose, role and objectives (full text)

Mandate. To support the involvement of parents in the educational programs of McRoberts Secondary School.

Purpose. To support school staff and work together in promoting the success of students in their educational endeavours. To promote a spirit of communication, tolerance and co-operation within the school community.

Role. To provide a forum for parental discussion, involvement and participation in school activities.

Objectives, in partnership with school administrators:

  • To communicate with all partners in education.
  • To support and promote effective educational programs and pursue the goal of improving education services in a healthy, safe and supportive environment.
  • To inform parents and provide a forum about programs and services.
  • To address the concerns and needs of parents, students, and school staff and make recommendations regarding programs and services.
  • To organize volunteer programs and fundraising endeavours which support student activities.
Who is a member

Every parent or guardian of a McRoberts student is a member and can vote.

School administrators, teaching and non-teaching staff, and community members without a current student can take part, but they do not vote.

Members are asked to come to as many meetings as possible, learn about the school's programs, and support the PAC's purpose.

The executive

The bylaws name four executive roles: Chair, Secretary, Treasurer and RDPA Representative. Their duties are listed under The {{site.schoolYear}} executive, above.

Where possible, one person holds one role, for no more than two years, and then advises the next person.

Elections

The executive is elected every year at the first meeting of the school year. The previous Chair, or the principal, runs the election.

Meetings and decisions

Quorum. To make decisions, a meeting needs at least two-thirds of the executive and at least five members.

Rules. Meetings follow Robert's Rules of Order, a common set of rules for running fair meetings.

Special meetings. The principal and parents get seven days' notice of a special meeting, except when the matter is urgent.

Minutes. The Secretary records the minutes and makes them available to members.

The principal's role

The principal helps organize the PAC, encourages parents to take part, reports on school programs, and comes to meetings or sends a staff member.
