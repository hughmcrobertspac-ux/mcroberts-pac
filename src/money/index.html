Money

Where the money goes

The PAC looks after money for McRoberts students. We want every family to see where it comes from, who decides how it is spent, and what it pays for.

In short

The PAC has two bank accounts. A grant from the Province pays for clubs, teams, trips and grad night. Family fundraising pays for scholarships and for thanking staff. Parents vote on spending at PAC meetings.

Two accounts, two jobs

The PAC keeps its money in two accounts at Coast Capital Savings. Each account has its own job.

From the Province

Gaming account

Money in: the BC Community Gaming Grant, $20 for each student every year, plus bank interest.

Pays for: the Teachers' Wish List (clubs, teams, trips and events outside class) and the PAC's support for Dry After Grad.

The Province sets strict rules for this money. Read the rules.

From families

Family fund (operating account)

Money in: family fundraisers and donations.

Pays for: things the grant cannot pay for, like Grade 12 scholarships and thanking school staff.

Why this money matters.

A committee of Grade 12 parents runs Dry After Grad and keeps its own bank account, separate from the PAC's two accounts. The PAC supports Dry After Grad with money from the gaming account. About Dry After Grad.

For safety, this site shows account names only. It never shows account numbers.

How money is kept safe

These are the rules for PAC money. They come from the PAC's bylaws and from the Province's conditions for the gaming grant.

  • Two signatures on every cheque. For the gaming account, the two people who sign cannot be related, and at least one of them must be on the PAC executive.
  • No cash from the gaming account. Grant money is never paid out in cash.
  • Parents decide. Spending is decided at PAC meetings. For example, on November 12, 2025, parents talked about each Teachers' Wish List request, and the PAC approved $19,428.36.
  • A report at every meeting. The treasurer reports the account balances at every PAC meeting. The balances are recorded in the meeting minutes.
  • A yearly report to the Province. The Province requires a yearly report on the gaming account. Two executive members must sign it, and one of them must be the treasurer.
  • Records are kept. Receipts, cheques and bank statements for the gaming account must be kept for five years.
  • Open books. The PAC's bylaws say its financial statements are open to audit.

The Treasurer and Co-Treasurer look after the accounts. See who is on the executive. Questions about PAC money are always welcome, at a PAC meeting or by email to {{site.pacEmail}}.

The BC gaming grant, explained

Every year, the Province of BC gives school PACs a Community Gaming Grant. For a PAC, the grant is $20 for each student. The amount is based on the number of full-time students at the start of the previous school year. The money comes from the Province's gaming revenue.

McRoberts PAC received $20,700 in October 2025.

The grant must stay in its own bank account, the gaming account. No other money goes into that account. The grant is run by the Province's Community Gaming Grants Branch, and it follows the same yearly steps.

The yearly steps of the gaming grant
StepWhen
The PAC appliesOnline, to the Community Gaming Grants BranchApril 1 to June 30

The PAC's financial year for the gaming account runs from February 1 to January 31.

What the grant can and cannot pay for

The grant is for activities outside regular class time that help students. These examples come from the Province's 2026 guidelines for PAC grants.

Can pay for

  • Student clubs and teams
  • Team and club uniforms, and equipment for extracurricular sports
  • Field trips within BC
  • Guest presentations and performances, like music, theatre or science
  • Student competitions, like debate, chess, writing or music
  • Student conferences within BC
  • Graduation events, including Dry After Grad
  • School spirit and family events
  • Student recognition, like certificates and trophies
  • Student publications, like the yearbook
  • Books, magazines and games for free time
  • Membership in BCCPAC, the provincial parent group

Cannot pay for

  • Scholarships and bursaries
  • Costs for teachers and staff, like meals, wages or training
  • Textbooks and other things used to teach the curriculum
  • Tablets, laptops, Chromebooks or smart boards for class
  • Things the school or school district must provide
  • Repairs to the school building, like gym floors or washrooms
  • Field trips outside BC
  • Cash prizes and gift cards
  • Fundraising costs
  • PAC office and admin costs
  • Donations to other groups, loans or old debts

The Province's lists are longer. Read the 2026 PAC and DPAC grant guidelines (PDF, 1.4 MB) or the Province's page on PAC grants.

Why we still need family fundraising

The grant cannot pay for scholarships. It cannot pay for thanking teachers and staff either. Only money from families can pay for these.

  • Grade 12 scholarships. In 2025–26 the goal was five scholarships of $500. Families raised enough for three.
  • Thanking staff. Family money pays for the Staff Appreciation Breakfast each June.

Giving is always optional. Every amount helps.
